TL-CAP-001 · capture durability and truthful save · Phase A · 2026-08-30 · design-only, no production change

Make every recording recoverable

One user problem. A person speaks a thought they could not stop to type — then the app can silently lose it. The recording is created in temporary OS storage; if the upload fails, Home shows a small transient error with no way to hear the capture again, no retry, and no record that it ever existed. Even when saving works, the app never says what is actually true: whether the thought is on the phone, in the account, structured, or readable by their agent — and the recorder stays locked for up to ~40 seconds while it finds out.

This is rung one of the product’s outcome ladder — capture without loss — and the canonical portfolio decision Mike named next. The promise “a spoken thought remains recoverable until durable save is confirmed” is currently made by the marketing and broken by the failure path.

The shared truthful lifecycle (all three candidates)

Every candidate uses one vocabulary — a capture (fragile) becomes a note in your plan (durable) — and one state machine:

  • On your phone — at stop, audio moves to a durable app-owned outbox before anything else. Survives quits, crashes, and offline.
  • Saving to your account… → Saved to your account — the audio is server-durable; the border/dot/row says so.
  • Structuring your note… → In today’s plan · readable by your agent (only claimed when MCP is connected; otherwise “ready for your agent”).
  • Waiting for connection — offline is a calm queue with an automatic resume, not an error.
  • Couldn’t save. Still on your phone. — recovery offers Save again (same capture ID, never a duplicate), ▶ Play, and confirmed Discard.
  • Structuring didn’t finish. Your audio is saved. — honest stall with one re-request action, replacing today’s endless spinner.
  • The recorder frees the moment audio is kept on the phone — a second thought never waits for the first.

Shared guardrails: no new gestures anywhere (every action is a visible 44 pt button); semantic Dynamic Type for all new text; one VoiceOver announcement per state change; Reduce Motion variants for every transition; audio kept on the phone until account-durable, with a storage-cap policy question flagged for the handoff.

Three candidates

Candidate A
Born in place

The capture appears instantly at the top of the notes area — in the exact spot the finished note will occupy — and ages into it. Dashed hairline = not yet safe; solid = durable. Recovery happens on the card itself.

Candidate B
The recorder holds it

Nothing leaves the recorder until it’s safe. Captures dock in a one-line tray above the record button and only move up into Today’s plan as finished notes. The plan never contains anything fragile.

Candidate C
The save receipt

A quiet sheet rises from the recorder and draws the journey as a literal throughline — dot by dot: on your phone → saved → in your plan → agent-readable. Dismiss it and the journey continues; failure waits for you.

Canonical fit

  • Backlog: TL-CAP-001, state ready_for_mock; this is the mock its next action names. Slice phase after this review: candidate.
  • Primary metric: share of recording_started sessions that reach recording_uploaded.
  • Guardrails: local storage growth (outbox cap policy) and duplicate-upload rate (idempotent retry is a hard invariant in all three).
  • Principles applied: voice before interface; show the transformation; preserve the user’s words (replay); quiet hierarchy; one role per item; blue means state, not decoration; native confirmations; gestures additive (here: zero new gestures); accessible calm.
  • Non-goals: no Home-hierarchy redesign (the rejected 2026082601 presentation is not reused and the restored Home is preserved); no task-home work (TL-TASK-001); no onboarding, provider, pricing, recording-limit, policy, or release change; no backend implementation in this phase.
  • Authority: Mike selects; this phase changes design mocks only. Implementation requires the Phase B handoff and remains Codex’s.
  • Unresolved facts: real upload-failure frequency (guardrail still collecting); server-side idempotency and re-process endpoints do not exist yet; internal build 2026082801’s journey unverified on device.
tandem stage · awaiting_mike_selection

Recommendation: Candidate B — The recorder holds it. It gives the durability promise a physical logic (“nothing leaves the recorder until it’s safe”), keeps the just-restored Home list completely free of transient state, puts failure recovery at thumb height where a driver can see it, and adds the least ceremony per capture. Candidate C is the strongest brand expression and would make the best onboarding-adjacent moment, but it adds a sheet to every capture; Candidate A is the smallest change but reintroduces transient content into the exact list Mike just had rolled back.

Selection is Mike’s: A, B, C, a hybrid (for example B’s tray with C’s receipt available on tap), or reject all three. No production code changes until then.
